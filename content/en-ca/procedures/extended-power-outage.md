> **Safety:** never run a generator, barbecue or fuel-burning heater indoors. Carbon monoxide is invisible and has no smell.

## As soon as the outage passes 2 hours

- Tell the emergency management coordinator, who decides whether to [activate the emergency operations centre](/en-ca/procedures/activating-the-emergency-operations-centre/)
- Note the time the outage began and the areas affected
- Check the municipal garage generator: fuel, oil, battery

## Starting the generators

- Municipal garage first: it powers the radios and the fuel for snow clearing
- Town hall next: it hosts the emergency operations centre
- Community centre if a shelter is planned, see [Opening a shelter](/en-ca/procedures/opening-a-shelter/)
- Record in the log the start time and fuel level of each generator

## Protecting people

- Take out the paper list of vulnerable residents, kept locked in the general manager's office
- Organize door-to-door visits, in pairs, starting with people who live alone
- Offer transport to the warming centre to anyone who needs it
- Never enter a home alone where carbon monoxide is suspected: get out and call 911

## Every 4 hours

- Refuel the generators and note consumption
- Update the message to residents
- Check the temperature in unheated municipal buildings

## When power returns

- Stop the generators in reverse order of starting
- Check computer systems and the network before resuming normal work
- Have every generator refuelled for the next outage
